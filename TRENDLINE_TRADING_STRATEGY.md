## Strategy Overview

The **M & W Trendline Trading Strategy** has the following characteristics:

- Simple to use
- Works on any timeframe
- Works on any pair
- Involves no indicators
- Allows you to enter high probability trades with higher accuracy
- Entry based on touch of trendlines
- Based on following the trend, so the odds are stacked on your side

---

## Long Entry (Buy Setup)

**Trend Direction: Uptrend**

| Step | Action | Color Code |
|------|--------|------------|
| Touch 1 & 2 | **WAIT** — draw the trendline | Black |
| Touch 3, 4, 5 | **ENTER** after the pullback | Red |
| At previous highs | **EXIT** | Blue |

- Points 1 & 2 are used to draw the trendline. You don't enter — you wait for the third touch.
- You enter on point 3, 4, 5 after the pullback.
- You exit at the highs of the previous swing points.

---

## Short Entry (Sell Setup)

**Trend Direction: Downtrend**

| Step | Action | Color Code |
|------|--------|------------|
| Touch 1 & 2 | **WAIT** — draw the trendline | Black |
| Touch 3, 4 | **ENTER** after the pullback | Red |
| At previous lows | **EXIT** | Blue |

- Points 1 & 2 are used to draw the trendline. Don't enter — wait.
- Wait for point 3, 4 to happen and then enter after the pullback.
- You exit at the previous lows.

### Key Observations

- Every time price bounces from the trendline, it's another opportunity to enter.
- We only wait for the **third touch** before considering the trendline valid. Points 1 & 2 just build the trendline.
- The trendline trading strategy allows you to get in at almost the beginning of a new trend.
- If you miss an entry, wait for a pullback before jumping in.
- On an uptrend — only look for **buying** opportunities.
- On a downtrend — only look for **selling** opportunities.
- **Always stick to the trend.**

## How to Draw Valid Trendlines

### Two Types of Trendlines

- **Upward trendline** (uptrend) — connects troughs (lows)
- **Downward trendline** (downtrend) — connects peaks (highs)

### Steps

1. Identify obvious peaks and troughs
2. Connect a minimum of 2 peaks (highs) → downward trendline
3. Connect a minimum of 2 troughs (lows) → upward trendline
4. Start the line from the left to the right

### Choosing Significant Points

- For **lows**: the one with more higher candlesticks on its left and right will be more significant
- For **highs**: the one with more lower candlesticks on its left and right will be more significant

### Outer vs Inner Trendlines

| Type | Description | Best Timeframe |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| **Outer** | Main trendlines from significant peaks/troughs | H1 and higher |
| **Inner** | Drawn inside outer trendlines | 30 min and lower |

### Key Points to Keep in Mind

- How to draw valid trendlines
- When a trendline is still valid and when it is invalid
- Which trendlines are likely to hold and which are not
- How to use Support & Resistance to your advantage
- Be aware when the trend is starting or ending
- Use technical analysis for confirmations — no indicators, just pure price

## Valid and Invalid Trendlines

- A trendline is **valid** if it is not intersected significantly and price continues to obey it.
- A trendline becomes **invalid** when it is intersected significantly — this could mean the trend has changed.

### How to Tell if a Trendline is "Intersected Significantly"

#### 1. Candlestick Close Position

- If a candlestick **closes above** a downward trendline by a significant distance → signal the trendline is violated
- If a candlestick **closes below** an upward trendline significantly → signal the trendline is violated
- If a candlestick just intersects but **fails to close** above/below → expect the trendline to be obeyed

#### 2. Candlestick Body Length

- **Longer body** that closes beyond the trendline → greater possibility the trendline has been violated
- **Shorter body** that intersects the trendline → market lost momentum; there's a good possibility price will continue to obey the trendline
- You need confirmation via **reversal candlesticks**

#### 3. H1 or H4 Candlestick Close

- The close of H1 and H4 timeframe candlesticks is very important in determining if a trendline will break
- H1 close above or below a trendline has **more significance** than any other timeframe
- You may see a trendline intersected on 5min–30min, but then the H1 closes and tells the real story
- Pay attention to the lengths of candlestick bodies around trendline entry points — they indicate market sentiment

### Pin Bar (Change of Trend)

- Look at the length of the candlestick bodies. A strong bullish candlestick with a very long body indicates strong upward momentum.
- Candlesticks that follow with much shorter bodies indicate the market is losing steam.
- Another confirmation: a breakout of the trendline.
- **Candlestick patterns are very important** — we don't use indicators, just pure price.
- Get familiar with your candlesticks to identify reversals vs continuations.

## Common Trendline Mistakes

### 1. Drawing Through Obstructions

- There should be **no price obstruction** between point 1 and point 2
- Do not draw a trendline through an obstruction (price action between your two anchor points)

### 2. Drawing Through Wicks and Bodies

- Beginners draw a line through the general direction of the trend
- If the line crosses lots of wicks and candlestick bodies, it does not give you useful support/resistance information
- This is the **wrong way** to draw a trendline

### 3. Not Touching Peaks or Troughs

- This is a major mistake most traders make
- The trendline **must touch** the 2 spikes/anchor points
- If you fail to do this properly, you'll have situations where the trendline is not touched and price moves away from it
- You'll miss opportunities and enter on false signals

## Strong and Weak Trendlines

### Strong Trendlines

- The more price touches the trendline, the **stronger** it is
- If price obeys a trendline more than once, it's a strong trendline
- **Gently sloping trendlines** are the most reliable and strong
- Always keep an eye out for multiple trendline setups on gentle slopes

### Weak Trendlines

- **Steeply sloping trendlines** are generally very unreliable
- The market cannot sustain steepness for a very long time
- For steep trendlines, price often obeys the trendline only once or not at all
- Price tends to find a lower level of steepness which can be sustained

### Rules for Steep Trendlines

1. If you're in a steep trendline trade — **take profit quickly**. Lock profits or move to break even.
2. **Don't rush** to enter a steep trendline trade, especially on the 2nd or 3rd setup. The market may reverse quickly.

## Support and Resistance

When we draw trendlines, we are operating on support and resistance principles.

### Bullish Market (Uptrend)

- The upward trendline provides **support** for prices
- Prices go down, touch the trendline, and bounce back up

### Bearish Market (Downtrend)

- The downward trendline provides **resistance** to price
- Prices go up and reverse back down from the trendline

## Combining Trendline S/R with Horizontal S/R

This is one of the most powerful techniques for high probability trades.

### Long Setup (Double Support)

When a trendline entry setup **coincides** with a previous resistance level that has now turned into support:

- Trendline providing support
- Previous resistance turned support also providing support
- **Two supports working for you at the same level**

### Short Setup (Double Resistance)

The opposite: a previous support level that was broken becomes resistance, coinciding with a downward trendline entry point.

- Previous support turned resistance
- Downward trendline providing resistance
- **Two confirmations to sell**

### Checklist When Entering Trendline Trades

Check if the entry level has:

- [ ] Horizontal support level
- [ ] Horizontal resistance level
- [ ] Broken resistance turned support
- [ ] Broken support turned resistance
- [ ] Do they work in your favor?

## The Trend is Your Friend

If the market is moving in a direction, it will continue until an **opposite force** occurs:
- Resistance levels
- Opposing trendlines
- News events

### What is a Trend?

A trend is the overall direction price is moving: **UP**, **DOWN**, or **SIDEWAYS**.

| Trend | Definition |
|-------|-----------|
| **Uptrend** | Consistent move higher — Higher Highs (HH) and Higher Lows (HL) |
| **Downtrend** | Consistent move lower — Lower Highs (LH) and Lower Lows (LL) |
| **Sideways** | Price contained in a range until breakout. A resting period before continuation. |

### First Steps When Opening a Chart

1. Identify the present trend
2. Determine if the trend is ending
3. Be aware of a new trend beginning

## How to Spot Trend Changes

### Uptrend → Downtrend

The pattern of Higher Highs and Higher Lows is broken with a formation of a **new Low that is Lower than the previous Low**.

> When a **Higher Low (HL) is broken**, it signals a possible downtrend.

### Downtrend → Uptrend

The pattern of Lower Highs and Lower Lows is broken with a formation of a **new High that is Higher than the previous High**.

> When a **Lower High (LH) is broken**, it signals a possible upward move.

### Combining Trend Changes with Trendlines

1. First a HL is intersected → possible downtrend signal
2. Price goes up and touches the downward trendline → a short setup
3. With the downtrend signal already given and the trendline setup happening, you should be **confident entering a short**

### Why This Matters

- Get into trades at the **very beginning** of a trend change
- Lock profits or trail stops under higher lows (or over lower highs)
- When the structure breaks, it signals time to exit

## Short, Medium and Long Term Trends

### Trend Classification

| Type | Timeframes |
|------|-----------|
| **Short term** | 1min to 30min |
| **Medium term** | H1 to H4 |
| **Long term** | H4 to Monthly |

### Trendline Hierarchy

**Long term > Medium term > Short term**

- Long term trendlines have more significance than medium term
- Medium term trendlines have more significance than short term

### When Timeframes Conflict

If a short trend is down but the medium trend is up, two things usually happen when the short-term move hits the medium-term trendline:

1. **Short trend violates** the medium term trendline → medium trendline becomes invalid, possible new trend
2. **Short trend bounces** from the medium term trendline and obeys it (higher probability)

### What to Do

- **Take partial profits** and let remaining lots run at break even
- **Move stop loss tighter** to lock profits
- Always maintain the **big picture** of long/medium term trends when trading short term

> **Trades based on long and medium term trendlines have a higher probability of success — meaning big profits.**

### Timeframe Tips

- Focus on currency pairs with lower spreads (EUR/USD, GBP/USD)
- Preferred timeframes: H1 and H4 — keep trades running for days or weeks
- Switch to smaller timeframes to get **better entries** on setups from bigger timeframes
- Use 15min or 5min as your "larger timeframe" when scalping 1min or 5min charts

## Multi-Timeframe Analysis

### The Best Way to Analyse Charts

Start from Monthly down to Hourly:

1. **Monthly** — draw trendlines on obvious peaks/troughs
2. **Weekly** — add more trendlines (use different color)
3. **Daily** — continue analysis
4. **H4 → H1** — look for entry setups

> Use a **different color** for each timeframe's trendlines for clarity.

### Process

1. Start with monthly → identify direction and key trendlines
2. Move to weekly → find approaching trendline touches
3. Once you know the big picture, drop to H1 or smaller for **entries**

### Important Notes

- You do **NOT** need to draw trendlines every day on the same charts
- If a trendline gets intersected → remove only that trendline
- Long and medium term trendlines stay until broken
- Bigger timeframes cover up many good opportunities in lower timeframes

## Trade Entry Rules

### Entry Timeframes

Preferred: **H1 and H4** for entries. Also switch to **5min–30min** for early entries on larger timeframe setups.

### Why Use Smaller Timeframe Entries?

1. **Early head start** — more profit if the market moves as predicted
2. **Minimize risk** — smaller stop loss sizes
3. **Better position sizing** — smaller SL allows larger lot sizes while maintaining the same % risk

> **Example:** H1 entry → 30 pip SL → 1 lot ($300 risk). 5min entry → 15 pip SL → 2 lots (same $300 risk, double the profit potential).

### Stop Loss Placement

- Place stop loss **behind valid support and resistance levels**
- Give the market room to breathe
- **Don't place stop loss too tight/close** — you risk getting stopped out frequently

### Risk:Reward Ratio

- The system naturally finds R:R above **1:3**
- R:R 1:3 means a trade gives you 3× the reward of what you risk
- Example: Risk $10, profit target $30

### Trading Discipline

- If the system says buy → **buy**. If it says sell → **sell**.
- Don't over-analyze. Follow the rules. Have confidence in the system.
- Never forget **multi-timeframe analysis** — for trendlines, candlestick patterns, and S/R levels.

## Trendline Entry Variations

Not all setups are textbook perfect. Sometimes trendlines get completely intersected and become invalid, but the market comes back later and obeys the trendline.

| Variation | Type | Description |
|-----------|------|-------------|
| **#1** | Long | Price returns to respect a previously broken trendline as support |
| **#2** | Short | Price returns to respect a previously broken trendline as resistance |
| **#3** | Long | Multiple support levels stacking — the more, the better |
| **#4** | Short | Previous resistance levels still influence even after trendline intersection |

> Variations 1 & 2 are opposites. So are 3 & 4. These don't occur regularly but pay to know.

## Summary and Rules

### Practice
- Trade this strategy on a **demo first** for 3–6 months

### Analysis
- **Multi-timeframe analysis**: Monthly → H1, know where opposing trendlines are
- Understand trends and when new trends start
- Pay close attention to opposing trendlines and horizontal S/R levels

### Key Rules

1. Long and medium term trendlines have **more significance** than short term
2. Do **NOT** set take profit targets — use **trailing stop** to lock more profit
3. **Add on** — take multiple trades on a trending move
4. Apply the **7 powerful reversal candlesticks** as further confirmation
5. Pay attention to the **H1 and H4 candle close** — above or below the trendline
6. Check for reversal candlestick formations before placing orders
7. Focus on **H1 and H4** timeframes — more reliable than smaller timeframes
8. **Consistency** is key — proper risk management + follow the strategy + follow the plan

### Profit Approach

- This strategy is **not** about 5–10 pip profits per trade (unless scalping)
- Trades last **hours, days, or weeks**
- Target **big profits** from big trending moves
- The trendline strategy is about capturing big moves and staying long in trades

### Skill Development

- Go over charts and draw trendlines using past price data
- Test your skill at identifying how price responded to trendlines
- Identify reversal candlesticks and where the market turned
