## Fundamentals — News

Forex fundamentals refer to **economic news releases** that impact currency pairs. The foreign exchange market is very sensitive to the flow of economic data.

### Key Economic Indicators

| Indicator | Impact |
|-----------|--------|
| Non-Farm Payroll (NFP) | Very High — released first Friday of each month |
| Interest Rate Decisions | Very High — central bank meetings |
| GDP Reports | High |
| CPI / Inflation Data | High |
| Employment Data | High |

### Rules for Trading News

1. Do NOT trade during high-impact news releases if you are a technical trader
2. Check the **economic calendar** daily (e.g., forexfactory.com)
3. Close open positions or widen stop losses before major announcements
4. Wait for the market to stabilize after news before entering trades

> **Key Insight:** As a price action trader, focus on technical analysis. Use the economic calendar only to know when to stay out of the market.

## Technical Analysis — Price Patterns

### Candlestick Patterns

Candlestick patterns are the language of the market. They tell you what buyers and sellers are doing and help predict future price movements.

#### Anatomy of a Candlestick

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Real Body** | Area between open and close price |
| **Upper Shadow/Wick** | Line above the real body (high) |
| **Lower Shadow/Wick** | Line below the real body (low) |
| **Bullish Candle** | Close > Open (typically white/green) |
| **Bearish Candle** | Close < Open (typically black/red) |

---

#### 1. Engulfing Pattern

A two-candle reversal pattern where the second candle completely engulfs the first.

- **Bullish Engulfing:** Forms at the bottom of a downtrend. The second bullish candle completely covers the first bearish candle → buyers have taken control.
- **Bearish Engulfing:** Forms at the top of an uptrend. The second bearish candle completely engulfs the first bullish candle → sellers have taken control.

> The engulfing pattern reflects a total shift of power between bulls and bears.

---

#### 2. Doji

A candle where the open and close prices are virtually the same, creating a very small or non-existent real body.

- Indicates **indecision** in the market
- Neither buyers nor sellers are in control
- Most significant when appearing after a strong trend
- Requires confirmation from the next candle

---

#### 3. Dragonfly Doji

A doji with a **long lower shadow** and no upper shadow. The open, close, and high are at or very near the same level.

- Forms at the bottom of a downtrend → **bullish reversal signal**
- Shows that sellers pushed prices down but buyers brought it back up
- The longer the lower shadow, the stronger the signal

---

#### 4. Gravestone Doji

A doji with a **long upper shadow** and no lower shadow. The open, close, and low are at or very near the same level.

- Forms at the top of an uptrend → **bearish reversal signal**
- Shows that buyers pushed prices up but sellers brought it back down
- The longer the upper shadow, the stronger the signal

---

#### 5. Morning Star

A three-candle **bullish reversal** pattern found at the bottom of a downtrend:

1. First candle: Large bearish candle
2. Second candle: Small-bodied candle (star) — shows indecision
3. Third candle: Large bullish candle that closes well into the first candle's body

> The morning star tells us that sellers are weakening, and buyers are starting to take control.

---

#### 6. Evening Star

A three-candle **bearish reversal** pattern found at the top of an uptrend:

1. First candle: Large bullish candle
2. Second candle: Small-bodied candle (star) — shows indecision
3. Third candle: Large bearish candle that closes well into the first candle's body

> The evening star is the bearish counterpart of the morning star — signals a potential top.

---

#### 7. Hammer / Pin Bar

A single candle with a **small real body** and a **long lower shadow** (at least 2× the body length).

- Forms at the bottom of a downtrend → **bullish reversal signal**
- Sellers pushed price down but buyers rejected lower prices
- The longer the lower shadow, the more significant
- Confirmation: Next candle should close bullish

> Also called a "Pin Bar" in price action terminology. One of the most reliable reversal patterns.

---

#### 8. Shooting Star

A single candle with a **small real body** and a **long upper shadow** (at least 2× the body length).

- Forms at the top of an uptrend → **bearish reversal signal**
- Buyers pushed price up but sellers rejected higher prices
- The longer the upper shadow, the more significant
- Confirmation: Next candle should close bearish

---

#### 9. Harami / Inside Bar

A two-candle pattern where the **second candle is completely contained** within the body of the first candle.

- **Bullish Harami:** Small bullish candle inside a large bearish candle at the bottom of a downtrend
- **Bearish Harami:** Small bearish candle inside a large bullish candle at the top of an uptrend
- Indicates consolidation/indecision before a potential reversal
- The breakout direction from the mother candle signals the next move

---

#### 10. Tweezers Tops and Bottoms

**Tweezers Top:** Two candles with matching or near-matching highs at the top of an uptrend → bearish reversal.

**Tweezers Bottom:** Two candles with matching or near-matching lows at the bottom of a downtrend → bullish reversal.

- Shows that the market tested a level twice and was rejected
- More significant at major support/resistance levels

### Market Structure

Market structure is the study of market behavior — understanding what the crowd is doing, who controls the market, and when to enter, exit, or stay away.

#### Three Types of Markets

| Market Type | Characteristics | How to Identify |
|-------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Trending** | Directional movement with HH/HL or LH/LL | Series of higher highs & higher lows (uptrend) or lower highs & lower lows (downtrend) |
| **Ranging** | Horizontal movement between boundaries | Price bounces between defined support and resistance |
| **Choppy** | No clear structure, lots of noise | Cannot identify clear boundaries or trend — stay away |

#### Trending Markets

- Characterized by **impulsive moves** (in the trend direction) and **retracement moves** (pullbacks)
- Trends occur approximately **30% of the time** — take advantage when they appear
- Use bigger time frames (4H, Daily, Weekly) to determine market structure
- **Buy at the beginning of impulsive moves** in uptrends
- **Sell at the beginning of impulsive moves** in downtrends
- Never buy during a retracement in an uptrend or sell during a retracement in a downtrend

> Professional traders always buy at the beginning of an impulsive move and take profits at the end of it.

#### Ranging Markets

- Price moves horizontally between **support** (bottom boundary) and **resistance** (top boundary)
- Buyers are equal to sellers — no one is in control
- Three ways to trade:
  1. **Buy at support, sell at resistance** — trade from the boundaries
  2. **Trade the breakout** — when support or resistance breaks, a new trend begins
  3. **Trade the pullback after breakout** — wait for price to retest the broken level

> **Warning:** Not all breakouts are real. Range boundaries are often overshot, trapping breakout traders. Always confirm breakouts.

#### Choppy Markets

- No clear direction, lots of noise, very small tight range
- **Do not trade choppy markets** — you will give back profits
- Zoom out on the daily chart to determine if a market is ranging or just choppy

### Support and Resistance

Support and resistance are **proven areas where buyers and sellers find equilibrium** — major turning points in the market.

- **Support:** Level where buying pressure prevents further price decline
- **Resistance:** Level where selling pressure prevents further price increase
- Price tends to respect these levels until a breakout occurs
- In trending markets, **previous swing points** act as support (uptrend) or resistance (downtrend)
- When resistance breaks, it often becomes support (and vice versa)

> By drawing support/resistance in a trending market, you can predict when the next impulsive move will take place.

---

### Trend Lines

Trend lines identify **linear support and resistance** by connecting swing highs or swing lows.

#### How to Draw Trend Lines

1. Find at least **2 minimum swing points**
2. Connect them with a straight line
3. The levels must be **clear and obvious** — don't force a trend line
4. Use **4H and Daily time frames** only — never use smaller time frames

#### Rules

- In an **uptrend**, connect the swing lows → trend line acts as **support**
- In a **downtrend**, connect the swing highs → trend line acts as **resistance**
- When price approaches a valid trend line, expect a reaction (bounce or break)

### Time Frames and Top-Down Analysis

**Primary time frames for price action:** 1H, 4H, and Daily.

> Price action works on bigger time frames. Trading pin bars or engulfing bars on the 5-minute chart will lose you money — there is too much noise on smaller time frames.

#### Top-Down Analysis Process

1. **Start with the Weekly chart** — gather:
   - Most important support and resistance levels
   - Overall market structure (trending, ranging, choppy)
   - What the last weekly candle tells us
2. **Move to the Daily/4H chart** — gather:
   - Market condition on this time frame
   - Key levels (S/R, supply/demand, trend lines)
   - Price action signals (pin bar, engulfing, inside bar)
3. **Align the analysis** — only take trades where the bigger and smaller time frame analyses agree

> If you trade price action based on a single time frame, you will end up losing your entire account. The top-down analysis is a critical step.

#### Example

A pin bar on the Daily chart may look like a buy signal. But if the Weekly chart shows price is at a major resistance level, the trade should be skipped or reversed → always check the bigger picture first.

### The Pin Bar Candlestick Pattern Strategy

The pin bar is characterized by a **long tail showing rejection** and a **small real body**. It indicates the market will move in the opposite direction of the tail.

#### Quality Pin Bar Criteria

1. **Time frame:** Only trade pin bars on 4H or Daily — smaller TFs generate too many false signals
2. **Direction:** Pin bars formed **in line with the trend** are more powerful than counter-trend
3. **Anatomy:** Longer tail = more powerful signal. The tail should be at least 2× the body
4. **Location:** Must form at a **key level** (support, resistance, moving average, trend line)

#### Trading Pin Bars with the Trend

1. Identify a clear uptrend or downtrend
2. Wait for a pullback to support (uptrend) or resistance (downtrend)
3. Look for a pin bar forming at that level
4. Enter after the pin bar closes (aggressive) or at 50% retracement of the range bar (conservative)
5. Stop loss: Beyond the long tail
6. Profit target: Next support/resistance level

#### Entry Options

| Method | Description | Pros | Cons |
|--------|-------------|------|------|
| **Aggressive** | Enter immediately after pin bar closes | Catch the full move | Slightly higher risk |
| **Conservative** | Enter at 50% retracement of the pin bar | Better risk/reward (5:1+) | May miss the trade entirely |

#### Trading with Confluence

Confluence means **multiple technical factors confirming the same signal**. The more factors align, the higher the probability.

**Factors of Confluence:**
- The trend direction
- Support and resistance levels
- Supply and demand zones
- Moving averages (8 and 21 SMA) as dynamic S/R
- Fibonacci retracement (50% and 61% levels)
- Trend lines

> One or two factors of confluence combined with a good pin bar is enough for a profitable trade.

#### Pin Bars in Range-Bound Markets

- Identify at least two touches of support and two of resistance to confirm the range
- Buy pin bars at support, sell pin bars at resistance
- Also trade pin bars after breakouts and pullbacks from range boundaries
- Use **Bollinger Bands** as additional confirmation (rejection from bands + horizontal level)

### The Engulfing Bar Candlestick Pattern Strategy

The engulfing bar pattern consists of **two opposite-colored bodies** where the second candle completely engulfs the first.

#### Three Criteria (Steve Nison)

1. The market is in a clearly definable uptrend or downtrend
2. The second candle completely engulfs the first candle's body
3. The second real body is the opposite color of the first

#### Trading the Engulfing Bar

Apply the same three elements: **Trend + Level + Signal**

1. **Identify the trend** — HH/HL for uptrend, LH/LL for downtrend
2. **Find the key level** — Support, resistance, supply/demand, moving average, Fibonacci
3. **Wait for the engulfing bar** at the key level in line with the trend

#### With Moving Averages

- Use **21 and 8 SMA** on Daily and 4H charts
- In uptrend: buy when price pulls back to the MA and forms a bullish engulfing
- In downtrend: sell when price retraces to the MA and forms a bearish engulfing
- MA acts as **dynamic support** (uptrend) or **dynamic resistance** (downtrend)

#### With Fibonacci Retracement

- After a strong move, use Fibonacci retracement tool to identify the **50% and 61%** levels
- If an engulfing bar forms at one of these levels → high-probability setup
- Trading from Fibonacci levels means you are trading from better price levels

#### With Trend Lines

- Connect swing highs (downtrend) or swing lows (uptrend) to draw trend lines
- When price approaches the trend line and forms an engulfing bar → trade entry
- Trend lines help catch trades that horizontal S/R would miss

#### In Sideways/Ranging Markets

- Trade engulfing bars **from the boundaries** (support and resistance)
- Trade the breakout direction when price leaves the range
- Trade **false breakouts** — when price breaks a level, then quickly reverses back:
  - This is one of the most powerful price action strategies
  - Allows you to buy bottoms and sell tops intelligently

#### With Supply and Demand Zones

Three factors define quality supply/demand areas:
1. **Strength of the move** — quick, strong departure from the zone
2. **Good profit zone** — adequate risk/reward potential
3. **Bigger time frames** — Daily and 4H zones are most powerful

#### Money Management for Engulfing Bar Trades

- Minimum **2:1 risk-to-reward ratio** — the potential profit must be at least 2× the risk
- **Case study:** 10 trades at 3:1 R/R, win 3, lose 7 → Losses: $1,400, Wins: $1,800 = **Net profit $400**
- Risk no more than **2% of equity per trade** (1% for beginners)

### The Inside Bar Candlestick Pattern Strategy

The inside bar is a **two-candle pattern**: a large "mother candle" followed by a smaller candle completely contained within it.

#### Key Statistics (Thomas Bulkowski)

- Bearish inside bar in a bull market → bearish reversal ~65% of the time
- Bullish inside bar in a bull market → bullish continuation ~52% of the time
- Bullish abandoned baby → bullish reversal ~70% in bull markets, ~55% in bear markets

#### Psychology

- Indicates a **period of consolidation and indecision**
- In a bullish trend: bulls are pausing before the next move
- In a bearish trend: sellers are pausing before the next move

#### Trading as a Continuation Pattern (With the Trend)

1. Identify a strong trend
2. Wait for an inside bar to form in line with the trend direction
3. Enter after the **breakout of the mother candle** in the trend direction
4. Stop loss: Above/below the mother candle
5. Profit target: Next support/resistance level

#### Trading with Support and Resistance

- When price breaks a support/resistance level and forms an inside bar:
  - The inside bar shows indecision — the breakout is not yet confirmed
  - Wait for the **inside bar breakout** to confirm the level breakout
  - This avoids being caught in false breakouts

#### Inside Bar False Breakout Strategy

This is when price breaks out from the inside bar pattern and then **quickly reverses** to close within the range of the mother bar.

- **Bullish false breakout:** Forms in a downtrend near key support → bullish reversal signal
- **Bearish false breakout:** Forms in an uptrend near key resistance → bearish reversal signal

> Banks and institutions use **stop-hunting** strategies to create liquidity. The inside bar false breakout pattern reveals this manipulation.

#### Key Levels for Inside Bar False Breakouts

- Support and resistance, supply and demand areas
- Fibonacci retracement (50% and 61%)
- 21 moving average and trend lines in trending markets
- Horizontal levels in range-bound markets

#### Benefits

- Enter when trapped traders are forced to exit → excellent timing
- High risk/reward potential (e.g., risk 50 points for 400 points profit)
- Helps predict turning points and understand institutional trading behavior

#### Tips for Trading Inside Bars

1. **Trade bigger time frames** — Daily and 4H (not 5-minute)
2. **Trade the dominant trend** — stick with trend direction, especially as a beginner
3. **Trade only from key levels** — not all inside bars are worth trading
4. **Find confluence** — combine multiple signals for higher probability

## Money Management Strategies

> The most important thing that traders don't talk about is money management. This is what makes the difference between successful traders and losers.

### Position Sizing

| Account Type | Value Per Pip |
|-------------|--------------|
| 1 Standard Lot | ~$10/pip |
| 1 Mini Lot | ~$1/pip |
| 1 Micro Lot | ~$0.10/pip |

- Think in **dollars risked**, not pips
- Calculate how much you may win and how much you may lose before every trade

### Risk-to-Reward Ratio

| Ratio | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| 1:2 | Risk $100 to win $200 | Win 5 of 10 trades → lose $500, win $1,000 = **$500 profit** |
| 1:3 | Risk $100 to win $300 | Win 3 of 10 trades → lose $700, win $900 = **$200 profit** |

> Never enter a trade where the potential profit is less than the amount risked. Minimum 1:2 R/R.

### Stop Loss Rules

1. **Always use a stop loss** — place it with your order on the platform
2. **Never use mental stops** — you cannot control the market
3. Place your stop loss and profit target, then **walk away**
4. Trade out of your emotions — accept that losing is part of the game
5. Risk no more than **2% of equity per trade** (1% for beginners)

### Capital Rules

- **Never risk money you can't afford to lose**
- Start small and build slowly
- Trading is a business — treat it like one
- If you trade scared, your emotions will control your decisions and you will fail
